<Video 1> is a guide to the camera path, timing and fixed studio lighting. The camera goes exactly once around one stationary figure at eye level, at an even speed and fixed distance. Start directly in front, pass the figure's right at one quarter, see the back directly at halfway, pass the left at three quarters, and return to the opening frontal view. <Picture 1> is the approved front appearance and <Picture 2> is the approved back appearance of an ancient gaunt medieval sorcerer in his late seventies: a tall sharply pointed crimson-red hood worn up over a pale, deeply lined face with a narrow aquiline nose, heavy dark brows and pale icy-blue eyes; dramatic upturned wing-like crimson shoulder ridges; a sweeping floor-length crimson cloak lined in dark red over a tall narrow jet-black robe; both thin pale hands clasped together at the centre of his chest round a tall straight dark wooden staff that stands down the middle of the robe to the ground, topped just under his chin by an ornate carved golden finial. Keep that exact face, age, hood peak, proportions, painted dark-ink and gouache style, costume and staff. The figure stands perfectly still, upright, with his hands clasped on the staff and the cloak hanging straight to the ground. The entire figure stays inside the frame. Plain flat grey backdrop with no floor, cast shadow or props. Only the camera moves. The figure does not turn, shift or sway. The lighting stays fixed in the room.
